01: The Problem

Cancelling a flight meant getting in line for a phone call

Costco Travel sells vacation packages that bundle flights with hotels and rental cars. Cancelling one meant calling an agent. Self service cancellation stopped short of anything involving a flight.

The call center fielded more than 1,200 calls a month about airline cancellations alone. During busy periods, members waited as long as seven hours to get through.

I asked a narrower question than "how do we add a cancel button." I asked why cancelling a flight was harder to explain than cancelling a hotel. The answer was airline credit. Unlike a refund, it comes with restrictions, expiration dates, and per passenger math that no one had designed for outside a phone call.

03: Research

I researched the call, not just the flow

I pulled the existing data on cancellation volume and call reasons. Then I partnered with our UX researcher and ran moderated sessions with the call center agents who took these calls every day.

The same pattern surfaced every session. Members were not confused about how to cancel. They were confused about what would happen to their money. Every call opened with a version of the same question, and agents had to answer it before they could do anything else.

That reframed the problem. Cancellation itself was easy to find. What was missing was a clear answer, before anyone picked up the phone, to what a member would actually get back. I designed toward that gap for the rest of the project.

05: The Solution

Four screens that finally explained the money

The research pointed to one job: make the financial outcome of cancelling legible before a member committed to it. Here is how that showed up across the flow.

Decision 01

Show the credit breakdown before asking for confirmation

Members needed to see exactly what they would receive, per passenger, before they could make a real decision. I moved the credit summary to the top of the cancellation confirmation screen rather than revealing it only after the action was taken.

Decision 02

Surface expiration dates as a primary piece of information

Airline credits expire. That detail was buried in the fine print across every prior touchpoint. I made the expiration date typographically equal to the credit amount itself, because for most members, the timeline is the thing that determines whether the credit is actually usable.

Decision 03

Build a per passenger breakdown, not a total

Groups travelling together often cancel individually. A single total credit figure was misleading when the credit was actually split across different passengers and airlines. I designed a per passenger table that matched how the credit would actually be issued and used.

Decision 04

Give members a way to save the credit details without calling back

After cancellation, members had no record of their credit outside of the confirmation email. I added a confirmation screen that displayed the full credit summary, copyable codes, and a PDF download, so members did not need to call back just to retrieve information they had already been told.

07: What I Learned

What I learned

Design toward the question, not the action
Members were not confused about how to cancel. They were confused about what would happen after. The UI needed to answer that first.
Call center agents are the best researchers you have
They hear the same questions hundreds of times a week. Getting into those sessions early saves weeks of discovery work.
Financial clarity reduces anxiety better than reassurance
Showing the number, not promising it would be okay, is what actually built confidence in the flow.
Post cancellation is still part of the experience
Members needed their credit details after the flow ended. Designing for that step prevented a whole secondary category of calls.

What I would do differently

I got into the call center recordings too late. The agent sessions were the most valuable research I did, but I ran them only after I'd already spent time mapping edge cases in the existing flow. Starting with the calls would have gotten me to the core insight faster.

I also should have pushed harder to recruit members who'd already cancelled by phone into usability testing. That group was hard to recruit, so I tested with a general sample instead. It missed anxiety points a phone experienced group would have surfaced.
